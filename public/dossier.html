To prevent subjective operator bias and generative hallucinations, CYPHER applies a rigid, multi-variable hazard evaluation rulebook. Every reported anomaly is mapped into one of five discrete triage levels with mandatory municipal SLAs:

Level Classification Formal Hazard Criteria & Triggers Target SLA Required Municipal Action
LEVEL 5 Critical Crisis Direct, un-isolated threat to human life or systemic grid collapse. Examples: Live 11kV electrical cables in flooded roadways, arterial bridge fracture, active toxic gas emissions, contamination of municipal water reservoirs. < 15 Minutes Instant emergency siren, automated substation interlock trip, multi-department tactical mobilization. Mandatory supervisor HITL override.
LEVEL 4 Severe Emergency Major structural breakdown requiring rapid engineering containment. Examples: Main road collapsed into deep sinkhole, ruptured 36-inch water main flooding multiple streets, high-voltage transformer fire. < 2 Hours Immediate deployment of certified heavy engineering units, road barricades, and public traffic diversion.
LEVEL 3 Moderate Hazard Substantial localized disruption with low immediate life safety risk. Examples: Blocked arterial drainage during monsoons, non-functioning multi-lane traffic signals, localized domestic water main pipe fracture. < 6 Hours Scheduled municipal crew dispatch within same operational shift. Standard field dispatch queue routing.
LEVEL 2 Standard Maintenance Localized non-hazardous structural wear. Examples: Isolated potholes, dark individual streetlights, broken sidewalk curbs, defective pedestrian crossings. < 24 Hours Routing to standard maintenance contractor backlog. Aggregated into daily neighborhood work orders.
LEVEL 1 Minor / Cosmetic Aesthetic or non-urgent neighborhood maintenance. Examples: Graffiti, faded road paint, broken public park benches, non-hazardous litter accumulation. < 72 Hours Low-priority municipal work order batching. Handled during weekly routine civic beautification patrols.

Semantic & Geospatial Deduplication Engine

During catastrophic infrastructure failures, dozens of citizens report the exact same incident within minutes. CYPHER automatically groups incoming filings into a single master ticket using a two-stage algorithmic barrier:

Step 1: Haversine Spatial Filter

Calculates great-circle distance $d$ between incoming incident coordinates and all active complaints in the municipal bounding box:

d = 2R · arcsin(√(sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat₁)cos(lat₂)sin²(Δlng/2)))

Where $R = 6371 \text{ km}$. Reports with $d > 0.10 \text{ km}$ ($100 \text{ meters}$) are immediately classified as distinct geographic events.

Step 2: 64-D Semantic Cosine Similarity

Reports within the 100-meter radius undergo vector similarity analysis comparing semantic intent:

Similarity(A, B) = (A · B) / (||A||₂ ||B||₂) ≥ 0.72

If Similarity $\ge 0.72$, the report is tagged as a Corroborating Duplicate (e.g. 🔥 7 REPORTS (1 PROBLEM) master pin). No duplicate crews are mobilized.
